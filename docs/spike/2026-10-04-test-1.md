## Finding that changes the spec: Genjutsu library provenance

The Genjutsu Trending/New "library" driving videos are **clips from real people's posts and music videos**: visible creator handles (@mocha_feb, bbok._.dori), an Instagram UI screen-recording, a performer's music video, a rap-duo performance, a woman's mirror-selfie video. The library is **not** a licensed/clean source.

Consequences:
1. The real protection is the **operation**, not the source: use `hf_mult_motion_control` only (output is our reference image's scene; only skeleton motion is taken). Never `hf_mult_replace_object` on third-party footage (it keeps their footage).
2. QA must check the output contains **no source pixels, people, watermark or audio**.
3. Never reuse a driving video's audio.
4. Cleanest source: **synthetic driving videos** generated by us (Seedance 2.5 t2v, 70 credits/10 s, includes its own AI-generated beat) → zero third-party content. Library clips remain usable as motion-only references for exact trend choreography.
5. The library's "dog" clips are quadrupeds (four-legged head-bobs) — they do not transfer to an upright character; upright characters need human drivers.

## Genjutsu test clip

Job `c15c43c3-4023-4c70-8242-be067a6935dc` — motion transfer, 1080p, dachshund master + synthetic driver, 110 credits, ~6 min render.

**Result: PASS (one cosmetic fix).**
- Source: native 1080×1920 HEVC, 24 fps, 13.8 Mbps, 10.04 s; **carries the driver's audio** (here our own AI beat).
- Master (spike `finish.sh`): 1080×1920, 30 fps, H.264 High ~14.5 Mbps, AAC 320k, **−14.0 LUFS, −0.9 dBFS peak**.
- Identity held across all frames (face, ears, coat, tracksuit). Motion transferred cleanly onto the upright dog (paw claps, arm waves, bounce); ears and fur move naturally.
- **No source content leaked**: our living-room scene, no dancer, no grey studio.
- Defect: the jacket/trouser gap shows bare belly when the dog moves → master references must use a **one-piece outfit** with no waist gap; visual QA gets an "outfit continuity" check.

## Consequences for the plan

1. Clean pipeline proven end-to-end: synthetic driver (70) + Genjutsu 1080p (110) = **180 credits per clip** (~$9). One biped driver can drive both the dachshund and The Butler → ~145/clip averaged. Library drivers (motion-only) cost 110 but need audio stripped.
2. Genjutsu output inherits the driver's audio → with a synthetic driver the soundtrack is already ours; with a library driver, **always replace the audio**.
3. Render time ~6 min per clip → the daily run must submit all clips first, then wait (batch), not generate serially.
4. Character stills: Seedream 4.5 high (1 credit). Dachshund needs an upright **and** a four-legged master (spec §4.4 step 3).
5. Budget re-check: 26 clips/month × ~160 credits ≈ 4,200 credits (~$210) — matches the spec's ≈ $210 envelope.
